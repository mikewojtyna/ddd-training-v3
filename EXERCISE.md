# Exercise 3: Archetype, entity or pattern?

**Module:** M6 Domain exploration & archetypes · **Time:** 20 min · **Form:** card sort, pairs · **Start:** tag `ex03/start`

## Situation

"Archetype" is used for very different things: a business shape, a class in the model, a design pattern, a workflow. If a team can't tell them apart, they look for archetypes in the class diagram and miss them in the business. This sort makes the difference concrete with words from your own domain: PLM, simulations and knowledge bases.

Your materials:

| File | What it is |
|---|---|
| `handouts/cards.md` | 12 cards, printable, one per row |
| `handouts/sorting-sheet.md` | four columns with one-line definitions, and the AI prompt for step 3 |

## Your task

1. **Sort (8 min).** Cut the cards and put each one in exactly one column: archetype, entity, design pattern or process. Don't use AI yet.
   For cards that fit two columns, write down the condition ("pattern if a developer says it, ... if the plant manager says it").
2. **Mark three cards** you were least sure about.
3. **Ask the AI (4 min).** Use the prompt from the sorting sheet.
4. **Argue (8 min).** Go through every card where you and the AI disagree. Pick a winner, or write down the condition under which each answer holds. Start with your three unsure cards.

## Done when

- All 12 cards are in a column.
- Every card that you think fits two columns has a written condition.
- You have at least one card where you kept your answer against the AI, with the reason.

## Debrief questions

- Which card did you move after talking to the AI? Which one did the AI get wrong, and why was it wrong?
- Which cards changed column depending on who says the word, the business or a developer?
- Where did an entity turn out to be just one element of a bigger archetype?
- Which card would you put on your own team's wall to stop a recurring argument?
