# Business archetype of a software engineering outsourcing agency

Source of archetype definitions: Neal Cabage, *Business Model Archetypes*, https://nealcabage.com/business-model-archetypes/

## Answer

A software engineering outsourcing agency is a **Service** business, and an almost textbook one. Cabage's own Service example ("platform customization and maintenance services") is essentially a software agency.

**Assumed scope:** the agency employs its own engineers and is responsible for delivering the work. Billing is time and materials, or a fixed price per project.

## How I tested each archetype

Cabage defines each archetype by two things: a **key activity** (what the business does to create value) and a **way of earning money** (what the customer pays for). For each archetype I asked:

1. What is the client actually buying?
2. Does the agency's main work match the archetype's key activity?
3. Does the way the agency bills match the archetype's way of earning money?

## Analysis per archetype

| # | Archetype | Cabage's definition | Analysis | Result |
|---|---|---|---|---|
| 1 | **Product** | One-time purchase of an artifact; key activity is product development | The client commissions custom work, and the resulting software belongs to the client. The agency doesn't sell the same artifact to many buyers. | **NO** |
| 2 | **Service** | Doing something for the customer and charging a fee; key activities are customization and maintenance; paid for time and materials | Direct match. Skilled professionals build, integrate, customize and maintain software that the client can't or won't do in-house. Billing is by hours, days or sprints. | **YES** |
| 3 | **Trade** | Connecting buyers and sellers; earns by buying low and selling high; key activities are sourcing and advertising | The margin between the client's rate and the engineer's salary looks like buying low and selling high, but the agency doesn't resell goods it sourced from others. Its value is organizing and standing behind its own engineers' work. | **NO** |
| 4 | **Subscription** | Product + Service with ongoing access for a recurring payment | Monthly "dedicated team" contracts recur, but the client pays for people's time, not for ongoing access to a product. It would be YES only with the agency's own SaaS or a productized maintenance plan. | **NO** |
| 5 | **Brokerage** | Trade + Service: trading on behalf of clients for a base fee plus commission | A pure body-leasing or recruitment shop that finds freelancers for a placement fee would be a broker. An agency that employs its engineers and is responsible for delivery isn't. | **NO** |
| 6 | **Marketplace** | Trade + Product: a self-service platform that recruits vendors and takes a commission per sale | The agency has no self-service platform matching clients with independent engineers for a cut. That's what Upwork or Toptal do. | **NO** |
| 7 | **Ecosystem** | Product + Service + Trade together, with partners and platforms | Only Service is present. Adding its own products and a partner or marketplace layer could move it toward an ecosystem. | **NO** |

## The judgment calls

- **Service or Trade/Brokerage?** The deciding question is who is responsible for the result. If the agency manages delivery and quality with its own people, it's a Service business. If it mostly passes third-party contractors through for a markup or commission, it moves toward Trade or Brokerage.
- **Service or Subscription?** Monthly dedicated-team contracts are recurring billing for people's time, not access to a product. Recurring billing alone doesn't make it a Subscription.
